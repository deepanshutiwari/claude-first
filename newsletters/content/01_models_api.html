Today you're starting the most important foundation of the exam: understanding what Claude is before you architect with it. Miss this and every downstream decision becomes a guess.

Claude comes in three tiers, and the choice between them is an architectural decision you'll make daily. Haiku is the speed demon — sub-second responses, the lowest cost per token, perfect for classification, routing, extraction, and any task where you need Claude to be invisible infrastructure. Sonnet is your workhorse — the balanced choice that handles 80% of real workloads well. Opus is the deep thinker — reserve it for complex reasoning chains, legal analysis, and tasks where getting it wrong is expensive.

The API itself has one core shape: a messages array where you alternate user and assistant turns. The system parameter sits outside this array — it's not a message, it's the frame. Every request also takes model, max_tokens (required), and optional sampling parameters.

Token limits are architectural constraints, not suggestions. Claude's context window determines how much history, documents, and instructions you can pass at once. When you're designing a document analysis system, the context window sets your maximum document size. When you're building a chatbot, it limits conversation history length before you need to summarize.

Version pinning in production is non-negotiable. Using claude-opus-4-8 instead of claude-opus-latest means a model update never silently changes your application's behavior. Always pin. Always.

Sampling parameters: temperature controls randomness (0 = deterministic, 1 = creative). For data extraction and structured output, use 0. For creative writing, try 0.7–1.0. top_p and top_k are alternative sampling strategies — in practice, just use temperature and leave the others at defaults unless you have a specific reason.

max_tokens is a hard ceiling. When Claude hits it mid-sentence, it stops. The stop_reason in the response tells you why it stopped: end_turn (natural completion), max_tokens (truncated), stop_sequence (hit a custom stop), or tool_use (wants to call a tool). Always check stop_reason in production — truncated responses are silent failures without it.

Rate limits are per-model, per-organization, and measured in both requests-per-minute and tokens-per-minute. Design for exponential backoff on 429s from day one. The pattern: catch the 429, read the retry-after header, wait that long, then retry. Never hammer on 529 (overload) errors — back off aggressively.

The model you choose on day one will be the model you're debugging at 2am on day 300. Choose deliberately.
